Refrigerator extension cord

Many manuals say use a grounded outlet, not an extension cord. If a cord is unavoidable, one short 12 or 14 AWG 3-prong cord rated for the nameplate.

Last reviewed: November 22, 2026

Many refrigerator manuals say plug the fridge into a grounded outlet, not an extension cord. If a cord is unavoidable, use one short 12 or 14 AWG 3-prong cord rated for the nameplate amps.

Do not daisy-chain a second cord, do not use a power strip, and do not run the cord under a rug. The manual that came with the refrigerator is the instruction that wins.

A cord set is listed to UL 817. The amps marked on the jacket and the plug are the limit. NEC 400.12(1) does not allow a flexible cord as a substitute for the fixed wiring of a structure. A cord that stays because the refrigerator has no receptacle is the substitute that NEC 400.12(1) does not allow. A new receptacle is the fix. This page does not publish one running-amp figure or one startup-surge figure. Nameplates differ. The table uses two planning loads from the extension cord gauge chart. One load is at or under 10 A. The other is a 15 A cord. Those loads are not measurements of a refrigerator. See the extension cord gauge calculator and the daisy-chain guide.

Do not buy that light-duty cord for a refrigerator compressor. Buy 14 AWG when the nameplate is at or under the amps marked on that cord, and 12 AWG when the nameplate or the start needs a 15 A cord. If the nameplate is above the cord mark, the cord is the wrong cord.

Follow the refrigerator manual

If the manual says not to use an extension cord, a heavier cord is still disallowed. When the refrigerator cannot reach a grounded outlet, the fix is a receptacle on that wall. Hire a licensed electrician for that circuit.

Questions

Can you use an extension cord for a refrigerator?

Many refrigerator manufacturers tell you to plug the refrigerator into a grounded wall outlet and not to use an extension cord. Follow that manual. If a cord is unavoidable, use one short 3-prong cord of 12 or 14 AWG whose marked amps are at or above the nameplate. Do not daisy-chain cords, do not use a power strip, and do not run the cord under a rug.

What gauge extension cord for a refrigerator?

Use 14 AWG when the nameplate is at or under the amps marked on that cord, and 12 AWG when the nameplate or the compressor start needs a 15 A cord. The voltage-drop chart would allow 16 AWG for a load at or under 10 A on a run of 25 feet or less. Do not buy that light-duty cord for a refrigerator.

Can a refrigerator plug into a power strip?

No. A power strip is not the grounded wall outlet the manual names. Use that outlet, or one extension cord rated for the refrigerator.

Can you daisy-chain extension cords for a refrigerator?

No. Two cords are a longer run plus another plug, and the thinnest cord sets the ampacity. Use one cord. The daisy-chain guide on this site explains why stacked cords fail.

How long can a refrigerator extension cord be?

Keep it short. This page sizes 6, 10, 15, and 25 feet. A longer cord adds voltage drop and is closer to using flexible cord in place of a receptacle. NEC 400.12(1) does not allow a flexible cord as a substitute for the fixed wiring of a structure. If the refrigerator cannot reach a proper outlet, have a receptacle installed.
